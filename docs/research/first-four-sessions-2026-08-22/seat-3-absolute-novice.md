# Seat 3 — Adult absolute novice (A1)

*Model: haiku. Panel: first-four-sessions, 2026-08-22. First person throughout — this seat reacts as a person, not an analyst.*

---

## S1: Meeting Slavko (Current Onboarding)

**What I hear:** Slavko's voice, warmly: *"Živjo! Kako ti je ime?"* (~3 words). Then a pause. Then: *"Me veseli! Jaz sem Slavko. Zdaj pa še ti."* (~7 words). Then: *"Lepo, da sva se spoznala. Adijo!"* (~5 words). English translations appear below each line. Generous silences between turns.

**What I see:** Slavko's face. A clear button I need to hold to speak. Text in English ("Practice speaking, then press the button to continue"). Nothing else. Clean. Not overwhelming.

**What I say:** Just my name. Then *"Sem [name]."* Then *"Adijo."* Three turns, four words total.

**What the app records:** Two learnable patterns (the "Sem ___" frame and the "Adijo" goodbye) and my voice saying them.

**Help on screen:** English gloss right there. Button clearly shows what to do. No score. No "you got it right!" — it just moves on.

**What's removed:** Everything except Slavko and the job at hand. No progress bar. No streak. No emoji reactions. It's literally just a person talking to me.

**Speed:** Slow. Generous gaps to think. About 90 seconds total. Feels like Slavko is actually waiting for me, not rushing.

**What it offers:** Two paths: (1) Watch Slavko have a conversation with someone who orders coffee ("Worked Example"), or (2) Talk to Slavko yourself in real time ("Free Chat"). I pick the first one because I'm terrified of the second.

## S2: The Greeting

**What I hear:** *"Živjo!"* Then I have to pick what to say back. Then Slavko: *"Kako si?"* (~2 words). English scaffold. Then Slavko waits.

**What I see:** Slavko. Below the button: two options in Slovene with English under each. I tap one or read it aloud.

**What I say:** One greeting. Then one response to "How are you?" — something real, not "fine" robotically. Then *"Adijo."*

**What the app records:** My greeting, my mood response, my goodbye.

**Help on screen:** English prompt. My two choices visible. Button labeled with the Slovene word so I can see/hear what I'm about to say.

**What's removed:** Absolutely no judgment of how I sound. No replay function to criticize myself.

**Speed:** Same pacing as S1. Still generous. About 60 seconds — slightly shorter because I'm doing less listening.

**What it offers:** Same choice: dialogue or free chat. This time the dialogue has someone greeting Slavko and answering "How are you?" — reinforcement of what I just did.

## S3: The Yes/No

**What I hear:** We do the hello/how-are-you exchange (now automatic, no on-screen cues — I just do it). Then Slavko asks whether it's my first time here (~4 words). He waits.

**What I see:** Slavko. After his question, the button shows two words: "Ja" (yes), "Ne" (no). That's it.

**What I say:** The greeting/mood (no scaffolding, I remember). Then *"Ja"* or *"Ne"* based on what's true. Then *"Adijo."*

**What the app records:** My answer. Not scored. Just recorded.

**Help on screen:** The two Slovene words with English below. Button is clear.

**What's removed:** The on-screen cues for hello and "how are you" — I'm expected to remember those now.

**Speed:** Onboarding pacing still, but slightly faster. Total time probably 50 seconds.

**What it offers:** Dialogue or free chat. I'm getting braver. I consider free chat but still pick dialogue.

## S4: The Job

**What I hear:** Greeting/mood exchange (automatic). Then Slavko asks what my job is (~3 words). Pause.

**What I see:** Slavko. Below: a frame that says *"Sem ___."* with a short list of jobs in Slovene — four, not twelve. I pick one or add my own.

**What I say:** The greeting and mood (remembered). Then the job: *"Sem študent."* Then *"Adijo."*

**What the app records:** My job statement.

**Help on screen:** The frame in Slovene. English gloss. The list shows both Slovene and English.

**What's removed:** Prompts for the greeting and mood. I'm expected to do that without help now. But the job is scaffolded with a list because I don't know how to say jobs yet.

**Speed:** Slightly faster. Total time 60–70 seconds.

**What it offers:** Dialogue or free chat. This time, there's a 90-second free chat option where Slavko asks me the same four questions I've practiced, but I have no on-screen help. It's just me and him.

## Five Answers (In My Voice)

### 1. The Stepping Stone

By the end of S1, I can say my name out loud in Slovene. Not in English. *Out loud*. It's tiny, but it's real. I've heard a real person ask for my name in Slovene and I've answered in Slovene. That's the thing I'd open S2 for: to do that again, but slightly more of it. Not "I learned five words." Just: "I can say my name to Slavko now."

### 2. The Frontier

Here's the trap: **I want to feel like I'm actually doing something (engagement), but I'm terrified of sounding stupid (confidence), and I can only absorb one new thing at a time (simplicity).**

In S2, if you dump "here's how to order a coffee," you've lost me. I hear 9 words, I have to produce 3, I have to remember which one's which gender, and I feel like a fraud.

In S3, if you ask me something new before I've had time to auto-pilot the greeting, I freeze. Cognitive load wins. I close the app.

In S4, if the list of jobs is 12 items instead of 4, I'm paralyzed. I don't know which one I can actually say. Simplicity gets sacrificed to look comprehensive, and I quit.

**What costs you:** You have to kill the impulse to add "one more thing" to each lesson. Every session feels thin to you (me saying 3 words while Slavko carries it). That's exactly right. Thin is good. Thin means I come back.

### 3. The Drop-Off

The app will lose me at the exact moment I finish a lesson, pick "Free Chat," and Slavko asks me something I don't recognize. If I've just practiced greeting and mood, and he asks me something with zero warning and zero prompt, I won't know if it's a yes/no question or if he's asking me to say a word. I'll hold the button, say nothing for 5 seconds, then hit a stall handler that repeats the question in Slovene slowly, and I'll panic. Three stalls later (15 seconds of silence), I'll mute the phone and not open it for a week.

**The exact moment:** Free chat, no scaffolding, no recognition of what's being asked.

### 4. What I Would Cut

The "worked example dialogue" after each session.

Here's why: I watch someone else order a coffee. Slavko's having a nice chat. I'm sitting there listening, and it feels like homework. I already practiced greeting (or whatever). Watching someone else do it makes me feel like I missed something, or I'm checking my work like a kid. It's passive. By S2 or S3, I'd skip it and go straight to free chat because at least there I'm *doing* something, not watching.

What cutting it buys: **90 more seconds of my time per session**. You'd be shocked how much that matters when you're nervous. And you free up the app logic for something that actually helps me — maybe a quick playback of *my own voice* so I can hear what I sound like, or a hint system in free chat so I don't stall out.

### 5. How We Would Know

You'd see someone like me **still using the app one week later**. Not because the app is addictive or fun, but because it doesn't embarrass me and it doesn't confuse me. Every session, I do *exactly one thing* that I didn't know how to do at the start. I record my voice. The app doesn't tell me I'm right or wrong. Slavko moves on warmly.

The second signal: **I'd pick free chat and not stall**. If by S4 I can go into a 60-second free chat, hold my own for 40 of those seconds (the last 20 I'd freeze), and still open S5 the next day, the system works. I'm not getting *perfect*, but I'm getting *brave enough to try*.

If you see me closing the app during free chat in S2, or skipping sessions, or even deleting the app after S1 — that tells you the step was too big, or the moment of confusion came too early, or I didn't feel safe.

**Confidence:** Medium — I'm inside the user's head well enough to know what would break engagement, but I'm guessing at pacing and the right level of scaffolding. A real test would be faster than this design.

**Strongest counterargument to my own view:** You might be right that one tiny thing per session makes me feel like progress is too slow. By S4, I'd only know how to say my name, greet someone, answer yes/no, and say a job title. I'd *still* be unable to order a coffee, ask for directions, or understand a Slovene friend. An adult might get frustrated and want the app to move faster — to cram more into each session so they feel less stalled. That would trade simplicity for momentum. I'd argue simplicity still wins (I'd quit if overwhelmed), but I can hear the countercase.
