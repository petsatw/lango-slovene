## Agent 1 — SLA acquisition researcher (opus)

**Recommendation:** Push the learner to speak inside the first minute — but *only* an unanalyzed formulaic chunk, and make it a **repair operator** (`Ne razumem`), delivered live into the AI tutor, so their first Slovene utterance visibly changes the tutor's behavior. Processability constraints govern *rule-generated* morphosyntax, not memorized formulae; there is nothing in a frozen chunk to fossilize. So the fossilization objection to early forced output doesn't bind here — it binds the moment you ask for a *conjugated* or *cased* form, which is exactly what I withhold. Front-load 25 seconds of dense comprehensible input, harvest one comprehension win, then convert it to output.

**Second-by-second script of the first run:**

- **0:00–0:04** Black screen, no UI. A Slovene voice: *"Dober dan."* Caption appears only after the audio, one line, in Slovene. Then the English fades in under it.
- **0:04–0:14** Three more utterances, each in a real café scene image, each transparently guessable from context and cognate prosody: *Kava?* · *Ja.* · *Hvala.* English captions trail each by ~1 second (hear → guess → confirm; this is where noticing happens).
- **0:14–0:22** A comprehension check that cannot be failed: audio *"Kava?"*, two big images (coffee / bread). Tap. Correct. One line of text: **"That was Slovene. You understood it."** No score, no percentage.
- **0:22–0:30** Mic permission framed as an invitation, not a form: *"Now say one thing back."* Native model plays twice — full speed, then chunked: *ne · ra-ZU-mem*.
- **0:30–0:35** Hold-to-talk. Learner shadows. **Acceptance is generous** — any recognizable attempt passes; ASR failure is never surfaced as failure.
- **0:35–0:50** Immediate live-tutor payoff: the tutor speaks a longer Slovene line at natural speed, the learner says *Ne razumem*, and **the tutor audibly slows down and simplifies**. The learner's utterance did work in the world. This is the whole onboarding in one beat.
- **0:50–1:20** Tutor offers one thing, in Slovene, with a binary spoken choice: *Kava ali čaj?* Learner produces **Kava, prosim** (or *Čaj, prosim*). One recast maximum, in character, as a question: *"Kavo, ja?"* Never as correction.
- **1:20–1:50** Second use of `Ne razumem` — engineered, unprompted, by the tutor deliberately going slightly too fast. Retrieval-before-display: the phrase is no longer on screen.
- **1:50–2:00** Tutor closes: *Nasvidenje.* Screen: two phrases, in production order, replayable. **"You can now do two things in Slovene."** No streak, no bar, no percentage.

**First utterance produced:** **"Ne razumem."** at **0:32**.

**Deliberately NOT shown:** Any alphabet, pronunciation chart, or phoneme drill. Any grammar label — case, gender, aspect, dual. Any conjugation (`sem/si/je`). Any word requiring the learner to *choose* an ending. Any mastery score, streak, level, or progress bar. Any English spoken aloud. Any written Slovene before its audio. Any lesson list or curriculum map — a syllabus makes the language look large; 30 seconds of success makes it look enterable.

**Key reasoning:**
- Formulaic speech precedes rule-governed speech in every naturalistic acquisition record. Frozen chunks are safe to force; morphology is not. `Ne razumem` and `Kava, prosim` are morphologically inert for the learner.
- A repair operator is the highest-affect first utterance available: it is the only phrase whose success the learner can *perceive directly* (the interlocutor changes behavior), and it permanently lowers the affective filter — they now know they cannot get stranded.
- Input must precede output *within* the same 30 seconds, not across weeks. The silent period's function is establishing form-meaning mappings; 25 seconds of ultra-redundant contextualized input does that for a 4-syllable chunk.
- Repair-first is also the panel's own ranked #8–#11 cluster (~100% transfer) — the onboarding should install the transfer-maximal items, not the scenario-bound ones.

**Confidence:** high — the chunk-vs-rule distinction is one of the better-attested findings in SLA, and it cleanly resolves the "forced production too early" worry rather than trading it off.

**Strongest counterargument to own view:** A learner whose very first Slovene utterance is "I don't understand" may internalize a self-concept as a non-comprehender, and the beat is affectively negative at the exact moment you most want elation. A Krashen-strict reading also says nothing about 30 seconds of input approaches sufficient intake, and that any pushed output at 0:32 is buying a felt win with a real ASR-failure risk that would invert the whole emotional arc. The safer alternative — `Dober dan` at 0:20, a pure social success with no failure mode — concedes transfer value but is more robust to a bad microphone.

## Agent 2 — Psycholinguist of speech production (opus)

**Recommendation:** Skip onboarding entirely — the live tutor *is* the first screen. Ears-first, eyes-late, mouth-open by second 25. The learner hears a native line twice before any Slovene text exists on screen, then produces one baked-case formula into push-to-talk. No menu, no lesson, no lobby.

**Second-by-second script of the first run:**

- **0:00–0:06** Black screen. Café ambience. One NPC voice, Slovene only, no text: *"Dober dan, izvolite?"* No UI at all.
- **0:06–0:09** One English line fades in: "Ljubljana. He's asking what you'd like."
- **0:09–0:13** Native model at natural speed, **audio only, no Slovene text**: *Eno kavo, prosim.* (Levelt: encode as a stored form, not a grapheme-to-phoneme assembly.)
- **0:13–0:18** Same line, chunked into three stress feet with a beat between each — `Eno · kavo · prosim` — English gloss appearing per chunk.
- **0:18–0:22** Slovene text finally appears, one line, stressed syllable marked. Hold-to-talk button pulses. Nothing else on screen.
- **0:22–0:30** Learner holds and speaks. Acceptance is deliberately loose: any /kav/ nucleus plus a request-shaped contour passes.
- **0:30** NPC answers *in character* — *"Takoj."* — cup sounds. Not a score, not a checkmark first: **the world responded.** That is the 30-second win.
- **0:35–1:10** NPC: *"Še kaj?"* Two tappable options with English parentheticals; picking one *plays* it, then the mic reopens to say it. Second production ~0:55.
- **1:10–1:40** One repair move installed under pressure: NPC speaks fast; a single button offers *Še enkrat, prosim.* Learner says it, NPC slows. The learner has now *controlled* a conversation.
- **1:40–2:00** Close: *Hvala. Nasvidenje.* Then, in English: "You just ordered coffee in Slovene." One button: keep talking.

**First utterance produced:** **"Eno kavo, prosim."** — spoken at **0:22–0:30**.

Phonetic defense: every segment maps onto an existing English phoneme or a harmless substitute. No č/š/ž, no palatal /lj ɲ/, no dark-l, no word-final devoicing hazard (finals are /o/ and /m/), no clitic to place, no Wackernagel second-position trap. The only cluster is /pr/, licit in English (*prosper*). Slovene /ʋ/ in *kavo* accepts English [v] or [w] with zero intelligibility loss. The trill in *prosim* survives as an English approximant — Slovene listeners parse it instantly. Stress is word-initial on all three words: the English default trochee is *correct here*, so no prosodic retraining is needed on utterance one. Five syllables in three separable feet means it is retrievable as one chunk yet breathable in three. And the accusative *kavo* is **baked in** — the learner outputs correct case morphology with zero morphological knowledge, which is exactly the felt-competence-without-cognitive-load trade we want cold.

The one tolerable error: English-speakers reduce unstressed final -o to schwa. Slovene reduces there too. Ignore it.

**Deliberately NOT shown:** The Slovene text for the first 18 seconds (retrieval-before-display; text triggers English phonics). Any grammar word — "accusative," "case," "gender." Any č/š/ž item. Any conjugated verb. Progress bars, scores, levels, streaks, the word "lesson." Red ASR correction of any kind. *Hvala* is withheld until 1:40 — one social token at a time.

**Key reasoning:**
- Articulator load, not frequency, decides utterance one. A first utterance that trips the mouth teaches "I can't make these sounds," which is unrecoverable.
- Felt progress = the *world* answering, not a checkmark. Put the reward in the NPC's voice.
- Baked-case chunks buy correct output at zero formulator cost — the only way a beginner sounds right in 30 seconds.
- Onboarding that isn't the tutor is a wall between the learner and the tutor.

**Confidence:** high — the phonetic argument for *Eno kavo, prosim* is inventory-level and near-unfalsifiable for English L1.

**Strongest counterargument to own view:** *Eno kavo, prosim* is a scenario-bound frozen chunk with near-zero transfer; the repo's own panel concluded repair-first (*Ne razumem*, *Lahko po slovensko*) because meta-operators transfer to ~100% of contexts. By spending the highest-attention 30 seconds of a learner's life on coffee, I buy a vivid win and a phonetically safe landing but teach almost nothing generative — and I risk the learner's second session being a fresh cold start on real material.

## Agent 3 — Slovene sociolinguist / pragmatics (sonnet)

**Recommendation:** Skip every menu. The first thing on screen is not a home screen with four destinations — it's a single live micro-scene (a bakery counter) that opens with cached native audio of a stranger-register greeting, gets the learner to reciprocate it within 15 seconds, and uses that reciprocation as the bridge straight into push-to-talk with the live tutor before the two-minute mark. The FELT win in second 15 is "a Slovenian said something to me and I said the right thing back" — not "I completed exercise 1."

**Second-by-second script of the first run:**
- **0:00** App opens directly into a portrait bakery-counter scene. No nav, no destination picker, no tutorial copy.
- **0:03** Cached native audio plays: *"Dober dan, izvolite?"* — vikanje, the real register a stranger uses at a counter, subtitled in Slovene only (no English yet).
- **0:08** A single pulsing push-to-talk button appears with one line of English: "Say it back."
- **0:12** Learner holds and says *"Dober dan."*
- **0:16** NPC responds warmly, in character, still entirely in Slovene — this is the emotional payoff: the app didn't flinch to English at an accented, halting attempt.
- **0:22** NPC's next line elicits an order with two tap-reveal choices shown as subtitles under the audio (rehearsal scaffold, not a quiz): *"Eno kruh, prosim"* / *"Eno štruco, prosim."*
- **0:35** Learner taps one, hears it, then push-to-talk repeats it aloud — second production.
- **0:50** NPC hands back change, closes with *"Nasvidenje."* Learner echoes it — third production, a genuine closing norm, not "bye."
- **1:05** Screen surfaces the pivot line, spoken by a friendly on-screen prompt, not the NPC: *"Want to just talk? Try: Lahko po slovensko, prosim? Se učim."*
- **1:15** Learner produces that line via push-to-talk — the "keep it Slovene" declaration.
- **1:25** Live tutor picks up immediately in Slovene, referencing the bakery scene the learner just did, and asks a simple eliciting question (≤7 words).
- **1:45–2:00** Learner answers freely, tutor responds again in Slovene — the learner is now inside real free chat, having produced five Slovene utterances and heard zero English.

**First utterance produced:** *"Dober dan."* at **0:12**. This is what a real Ljubljančan actually says and hears — it is the mandatory reciprocal half of a stranger-register greeting, requires zero grammar, zero conjugation, is impossible to get "wrong," and is exactly what happens at every counter in Slovenia. It is not a phrasebook opener like "Živjo, kako si?" (wrong register for a stranger) and not "Pozdravljeni" (stilted/written). Reciprocating the vikanje greeting is the single lowest-risk, highest-authenticity thing a beginner can produce.

**Deliberately NOT shown:** the four-destination home shell, level/variant tabs, any progress bar or mastery score, the A1 readiness map, grammar explanations (ti/vi, cases), and English-first captions. All of these are real MVP surfaces but none belong in the first two minutes — they read as homework, not immersion, and the first two minutes must feel like a conversation, not an app.

**Key reasoning:**
- Register accuracy is the credibility test: a stranger-facing scene must open in vikanje (*Dober dan, izvolite*), never *Živjo* — using the wrong register in the very first line would out the app as textbook Slovene to anyone who's actually lived here.
- The NPC never switching to English on a halting first attempt is the single most important trust signal for an English-speaking beginner living in/moving to Slovenia — it directly contradicts the real-world reflex they'll otherwise face on the street, and demonstrating that inverse is worth more than any UI polish.
- *"Lahko po slovensko, prosim? Se učim."* is the panel's #1-by-lived-ROI phrase for exactly this reason — it is the natural bridge from rehearsal into live production because it's the actual pragmatic move a learner needs on the street, not a demo convenience.
- Closing norms matter as much as openers — *Nasvidenje* said and heard in the first two minutes teaches an authentic close, something phrasebooks bury on page 40.

**Confidence:** high — the register choices, the anti-English-switch mechanic, and the greeting/closing pair are all things a Ljubljana native would immediately recognize as right or immediately flag as phrasebook-wrong.

**Strongest counterargument to own view:** A behavioral-habit designer would argue that skipping the home shell and going straight into a live NPC scene with no orientation risks a disoriented first-time user who doesn't understand *why* they're in a bakery or what app this even is — some minimal framing chrome (even three words: "Rehearse a real moment") might be needed before audio fires, or a portion of new users will feel dropped into a scene rather than pulled into one.

**Confidence:** high — the ASR-robustness ranking and the "don't score first contact" conclusion are well-supported by known Whisper-class behavior on low-resource languages; the exact seed script content is a smaller bet.
